Project 01 · GEOG 5530 Remote Sensing & Image Analysis

Identifying Urban Sprawl in Collin County, Texas (2015–2025)

Landsat 8 OLI · Supervised Classification · Change Detection · ArcGIS Pro

Collin County ranked 2nd in the U.S. for single-year numeric population gain in 2024–2025, growing from 144,000 (1990) to an estimated 1.3 million today. This study applies supervised maximum likelihood classification to Landsat 8 imagery across two time points to map where, how fast, and in what spatial pattern built-up land is replacing agricultural and natural cover across the county's full 2,296 km² extent.

Research Question

Where and at what rate is agricultural and natural land converting to urban impervious surface across one of America's fastest-growing counties?

Methods

Supervised MLC and unsupervised Iso Cluster classification on summer-season Landsat 8 composites; stratified random accuracy assessment (60 pts); binary change detection via Reclassify + Combine.

Key Finding

Built-up land expanded from 472 km² (20.6%) to 988 km² (43.0%) — a net gain of 516 km² in one decade. Sprawl is multi-nodal and most intense around Celina, Prosper, Anna, and Frisco along major highway corridors.

Planning Relevance

Provides a reproducible remote sensing framework for stormwater, urban heat, and infrastructure planning as the county moves toward a projected 2.2M residents by 2050.

Project 02 · GEOG 5550 Advanced GIS

Assessing Transit Accessibility in the City of Denton, Texas

ArcGIS Pro · Near Tool · DCTA GTFS · ACS Demographics · Equity Analysis

With Denton's population surging past 139,000 — driven by UNT and TWU enrollment and DFW suburbanization — the DCTA network serves a dense urban core but leaves much of the city's outer residential areas effectively unserved. This study classifies all 104 Census block groups into four accessibility tiers and cross-references findings with ACS zero-vehicle household and poverty data to reveal where transit need and supply diverge most sharply.

Research Question

Are DCTA's bus stops equitably distributed across Denton's 104 Census block groups, particularly for low-income and zero-vehicle households?

Methods

Near Tool (Euclidean distance from block group centroid to nearest DCTA stop); Spatial Join for stop counts; four-tier classification via Python in Field Calculator; ACS demographic overlay.

Key Finding

Transit supply is heavily concentrated in a small urban core (median = 0 stops/BG, skewness = 2.89). Several outer residential block groups with elevated zero-vehicle rates fall in the Transit Desert tier — transit-dependent but transit-poor.

Policy Relevance

Directly critiques DCTA's 2024 GoZone restructuring, which removed on-demand microtransit from Denton — precisely the service model suited to serve outer Transit Desert block groups this analysis identifies.
